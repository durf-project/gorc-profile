## What stood out

- **The social elements come first.** For a federation of existing systems, governance, rules and sustainability matter more than technology. The GORC model's ICT element is largely out of scope for us.
- **Resilience is assumed, not proven.** The information chain gives redundancy in principle, but recovery has never been tested and nobody monitors whether a record exists in all places.
- **The gap is in agreements, not in will.** The parties already work together, but on goodwill. Writing down roles and reciprocity is the most important step.
- **Where we need most support:** a funding model after 2030, a governance structure that includes non-UKB members, and capacity at smaller institutions.

### What was missing from the model

- The GORC model is written for a commons that is one organisation. A **federation** of autonomous institutions, nested in international infrastructures (Ostrom 8), needs more guidance on shared governance across organisations.
- Ostrom's design principles fit our situation well; we mapped them to the elements ourselves.
- We adapted the model by scoring it with the GORC v1.1 framework of the SPII tool (55 items instead of about 290) and by marking ICT, research data and software as not applicable.
