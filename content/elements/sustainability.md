Sustainability covers how the commons stays viable: funding and resourcing, stewardship of the research objects and services over the long term, and the trust of the community. The corpus will only last if it is clearly used, and if its services have the people and the money to be maintained.

:::ist
### Now: project money and goodwill

- The DURF project funds the coordination and development work until May 2030.
- Each institution pays for its own repository or CRIS. OpenAIRE's services rest on European projects and memberships.
- Redundancy exists in principle (source, OpenAIRE Graph, e-Depot) but is not managed or tested as one system. Two thirds of the records in the repositories have no file, so for them there is nothing to preserve.
- Trust between the people involved is high, built over years of DARE, NARCIS and the community groups, but it rests on goodwill, not on agreements.
:::

:::soll
### Target: a solidarity model before the project ends

- The UBs and the KB **jointly fund** the shared infrastructure: the OpenAIRE subscription, the functional and editorial management of the Netherlands Research Portal, and the coordination of the federation. Large and small institutions contribute in proportion; in-kind work counts.
- **Explicit redundancy:** every record and full text exists at the source, in the Graph and in the e-Depot, with a URN:NBN. The dashboards show this per institution. The restore procedure is tested at least once.
- The funding model is agreed in the general assembly well before May 2030, so the federation does not stop with the project.

**Help shape this:** how should costs be shared between large and small institutions? What counts as an in-kind contribution?
:::
