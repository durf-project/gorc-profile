Human capacity is about the people: those who run the commons, those who contribute to it and those who use it, and the skills, time and working conditions they need. In DURF this goes further than the people who maintain the systems. The commons runs on **in-kind contributions**: every member institution gives time and expertise, and each role, from library director to expert, has its own part to play. GORC asks for documented processes and succession plans, so that the commons does not depend on a few people.

The contributions below come from the in-kind needs per activity in the [DURF roadmap](https://durf-project.github.io/durf-gantt/) and the operational commitments of the [position paper](https://durf-project.github.io/position-paper/). Some are essential, others optional; some are needed during the project, others also after it.

| Role at the member institution | Expected in-kind contribution |
|---|---|
| **Library director or mandated person** | Signs the position paper and the Federation Rule Book, endorses the governance framework, takes part in the General Assembly at the annual Federation Day, and commits budget for the shared infrastructure after the project. |
| **Chief open science officer** | Reviews the commitments of the position paper and helps the institution take them on. |
| **NaMeCo representative** | Represents the institution in the Dutch OpenAIRE National Membership Consortium (four meetings a year), and reviews the Federation Rule Book and the governance framework. |
| **DURF coordinator** | Organises the institution's in-kind contributions, and keeps track of who does what. |
| **Repository and CRIS managers** | Keep the systems available for harvesting, implement the new application profile, add full texts within the agreed scope, deliver to the e-Depot and record the URN:NBN, and test enrichments and statistics pilots. |
| **Metadata experts** | Take part in the NL Research Information Content Board and its working groups, help draft the application profiles (3 to 4 half days and about 48 hours of preparation), and write best practices for their own system. |
| **Research intelligence teams** | Monitor and improve the coverage of ROR, ORCID and DOI, and support local implementation. |
| **Licensing, copyright and legal experts** | Help draft the legal structure for CC0 metadata in the UKB Licences working group, use it in publisher negotiations, and address the legal side of full text capture. |
| **Portal committee members** | Take a share in the editorial care and functional management of the Netherlands Research Portal, and join the OpenAIRE Connect working group. |
| **KB** | Preserves and resolves the metadata and full texts the libraries deliver, and keeps the URN:NBN resolver running. |

:::ist
### Now: contributions asked, not yet organised

The roadmap names an in-kind contribution for almost every activity, and the position paper turns the main ones into commitments. But for most institutions it is not yet clear who takes on which role, how much time that asks, or how the contributions add up across the federation. The people who do the work are known within their institutions and in the existing groups (WISH, PUGNL, the UKB working groups), but there is no overview across the federation, and much of the knowledge sits with a few people. Smaller institutions often lack the capacity to implement new standards. Contributions in kind are not yet recognised as contributions to the commons.
:::

:::soll
### Target: every role knows its contribution

- **In-kind contributions are part of membership.** The Federation Rule Book says per role what each member contributes, how much time it asks, and which contributions are essential and which optional, both during and after the project.
- A **public register of roles**: for each member, who fills each role in the table above, with a backup, so knowledge does not leave with one person.
- Each institution has a **coordinator** who keeps track of its in-kind contributions, and the federation reports them each year at the Federation Day, so every contribution is seen and valued.
- The skills each role needs are described in the rule book, and **peer support and train-the-trainer** sessions per theme help larger institutions support smaller ones.

**Help shape this:** which roles does your institution fill now, which contributions can it make, and which are missing?
:::
