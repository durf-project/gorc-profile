ICT infrastructure covers the network, compute, storage, operating systems and authentication that the services run on. Where the services run matters for **digital autonomy**: the corpus should stay under public control. But the choice of infrastructure belongs to each party, and it is not in the scope of the DURF project.

:::ist
### Now: each party runs its own

Repositories and CRIS systems run at the institutions or with their vendors, the Graph at OpenAIRE, the e-Depot at the KB. Several institutions depend on commercial CRIS platforms, which is a concern for digital autonomy. Storage is distributed, which helps resilience, but nobody monitors whether a record exists in all places.
:::

:::soll
### Target: outside scope, with one exception

The federation does not prescribe infrastructure. It does state a preference for open infrastructure that follows the [Principles of Open Scholarly Infrastructure (POSI)](https://openscholarlyinfrastructure.org/). The one thing it does monitor is **storage redundancy**: for each record, whether it exists at the source, in the Graph and in the e-Depot.
:::
