## Context and purpose

### Why we use GORC

We want to work towards a Dutch Repository Federation, in which the Dutch research libraries are responsible, together, for the custodianship of the Dutch Scholarly Communication Corpus: all publicly funded, published scholarly works.

Today there are individual repositories, many of them created in the DARE programme. They do not share a common purpose or a shared responsibility, because each institution works on its own and values its autonomy. The corpus exists only virtually, as the sum of the contents of all institutional repositories and CRIS systems. The metadata, and the full text files, can be brought together through the harvesting infrastructure of OpenAIRE, and backed up in the e-Depot of the KB National Library, which gives a second, independent way to resolve and restore them. The corpus can be searched through the OpenAIRE Graph API and the OpenAIRE Connect portal, for now at [netherlands.openaire.eu](https://netherlands.openaire.eu).

These services are crucial for the resilience of the corpus. **Resilience and redundancy are the main focus**: if a repository, the e-Depot, a publisher or OpenAIRE disappears, the corpus must not be lost, and the federation must be able to get the missing parts running again.

This whole mechanism, and its governance, is not explicit. Recovery has never been tested. Everyone assumes it will work and that they can count on each other, but there are no signed agreements on the standards, on how we organise ourselves, on the rules of participation and access, on solidarity, or on how conflicts are resolved. The GORC model helps us to describe all of this in one place.

### How we make the profile

DURF theme 1 (Governance) organises the profile. Consensus has to be reached among the cooperation partners of the DURF project, but writing it together from scratch would take too much time. So a small team makes the first draft and brings in as much as possible from the other themes: metadata quality (theme 2), full text capture (theme 3), e-Depot archiving (theme 4), discoverability (theme 5) and the portal (theme 6).

The starting point is the [DURF position paper](https://durf-project.github.io/position-paper/), which the research library directors of the cooperation partners are asked to sign. The status of each element is assessed twice, for IST and SOLL, with the GORC v1.1 framework of the [SPII Principles Alignment Tool](https://surf-ori.github.io/spii-1b-principles-alignment-tool/#gorc).

### Mission, mandate and stakeholders

The Dutch university libraries work together in [UKB](https://ukb.nl/en/about-ukb/mission-and-ambitions/). UKB aims to support and accelerate scientific progress by promoting and facilitating sustainable (open) knowledge for all, and to ensure reliable knowledge that is permanently freely available. It cooperates on sustainably accessible scientific collections, shared knowledge infrastructure, open knowledge, user applications and skills.

The DURF project is mandated by the rectors of the Dutch universities to make possible the strategic goal of their [Open Science Agenda](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10362464) on Open Science infrastructure, spearhead 1: *make a federation of repositories possible*. It is funded through the Open Science NL Open Infrastructure call.

The main stakeholders are the 13 university libraries, the KB National Library and OpenAIRE as core parties; the other Dutch research performing organisations (UKB associate members, university medical centres, universities of applied sciences); SURF; the community groups of repository and CRIS managers; Universities of the Netherlands; and the users of the corpus: researchers, policy makers, journalists and the public.
