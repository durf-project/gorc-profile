## Shared vision

The [DURF position paper](https://durf-project.github.io/position-paper/) is the shared vision behind this profile, and the background of the governance section.

> **Dutch Repository Federation:** together we, the libraries, are the custodians of the Dutch Scholarly Communication Corpus, containing 3.4 million publicly funded, published scholarly works from the early 1800s until today.

> **Mission:** joining this federation makes each of us responsible, together, to maintain the services and pieces of a federative Open Science infrastructure for Dutch publicly funded scholarly output, so that the Corpus and its services remain open, findable, accessible, available, and resilient in times of commercial and geopolitical turbulence.

### The corpus as a commons

The corpus is a shared resource because it was created with public money: what the public has paid for should be available to the public. No single institution owns it. Every institution contributes to it and every institution depends on it. Like any commons, it only stays healthy when the community that uses it also takes care of it: agreeing together what good looks like, measuring it in the open, and each doing our part at the source.

Elinor Ostrom found eight design principles shared by commons that last. They return throughout this profile:

1. **Clear boundaries**: which works belong to the corpus, and which institutions take part. See [Research objects](#research-objects) and [Services and tools](#services).
2. **Rules that fit local conditions**: agreements that work with each institution's own systems. See [Standards and conventions](#standards).
3. **Collective choice**: the parties make and change the rules together. See [Governance](#governance).
4. **Monitoring**: the state of the corpus is measured in the open. See [Interoperability](#interoperability).
5. **Graduated sanctions**: a signal and help first, firmer steps only if a problem persists. See [Rules of participation](#rules).
6. **Conflict resolution**: quick and accessible ways to settle disagreements.
7. **Recognition of the right to organise**: institutional boards and national bodies respect the federation's own arrangements.
8. **Nested enterprises**: each institution looks after its part, the federation coordinates nationally, and the corpus connects to international infrastructures such as OpenAIRE.

### Who takes care of the corpus

The DURF Federation sits in the middle: the university libraries and other institutions that cooperate in DURF, together with the KB, SURF and OpenAIRE. Around it are the DURF project and the community groups where the daily work is done. Point at a circle to see who takes part.
